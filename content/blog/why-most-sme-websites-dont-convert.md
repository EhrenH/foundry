Every week I look at small business websites for potential clients. Most of them look fine. Reasonable design, some photos, a contact form. But almost none of them are built to actually convert a visitor into a customer.

Here's the problem: looking good and performing well are two different things.

## The gap most businesses don't notice

A visitor lands on your website with a question: *can this business solve my problem?* They're not reading your "About Us" page. They're not filling in your contact form yet. They're scanning — and within about eight seconds, they've decided whether to stay or leave.

Most SME websites fail at this scan. Not because they're ugly, but because:

- The value proposition is buried (or missing entirely)
- There's no obvious next step
- The site doesn't answer the question the customer actually has
- Trust signals are absent or unconvincing

## What a converting website actually looks like

The best small business websites I've seen share a few things in common:

**They lead with the outcome, not the service.** "More bookings, starting this week" beats "Professional driving instruction." Same service. Completely different signal to the visitor.

**They make the next step obvious.** One CTA. Not four. Not a contact form buried at the bottom. One button, above the fold, that says exactly what happens when you click it.

**They use social proof early.** Testimonials don't belong on a separate page. The first one should appear before the fold, close to the CTA.

**They're built for mobile.** In South Africa, most website traffic is mobile. If your site requires pinching and zooming, you're losing customers before they even read your headline.

## What to do about it

You have a few options:

1. **Audit your current site** against these principles. Sometimes small copy changes move the needle significantly.
2. **Rebuild it properly.** If the underlying structure is wrong, no amount of copy tweaking fixes it.
3. **Measure.** Install Google Analytics (free). Set up a goal for your contact form. Watch where people drop off.

If you're not sure which applies to your business, that's what a discovery call is for.

The goal isn't a website you're proud of. It's a website that works.
